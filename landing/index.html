How it works

One HTTP call from your checkout. No dashboard to babysit, no rules to write, nothing to host.

1

It learns each customer

A running mean and variance per account, updated on every order. A customer who always spends 40 is judged against 40, not against everyone.

2

It decides in milliseconds

Unusual amount, unusual speed, or the model disagreeing. You get back approve, review, or decline — with the reason attached.

3

It tells you once

One message per incident, straight to Telegram. Repeats inside the same incident are rolled up, not re-sent at three in the morning.

What it does and doesn't do

Measured on 3,000 real transactions containing 88 real frauds.

What it's good at

  • Surfacing the most anomalous orders for review
  • Spotting card testing — bursts of tiny charges that look innocent one at a time
  • Judging an amount against the right yardstick
  • Not alerting twice for the same incident

What it isn't

  • It catches about a third of fraud. Most still gets through.
  • It's triage, not prevention — it prioritises what a human should look at
  • It needs about 30 orders per customer before its judgement is worth much
  • Every number above is reproducible, and published rather than rounded up
